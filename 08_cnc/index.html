<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3>Week 8: CNC Milling</h3> <h4>Assignment: Make Something With CNC</h4> For CNC week, I decided to CNC an ocean sunfish then cast it out of plaster with a pushpin inside to make my own custom pushpin. As a reminder, here's what an ocean sunfish looks like. <figure> <img src="../Fred Hsu Mola_mola_ocean_sunfish_Monterey_Bay_Aquarium_1.jpg" alt="Image of an Ocean Sunfish, a round, dinner plate shaped fish that is very flat and has a large, triangular fin on its top and bottom that it flaps to move. Photo taken by Fred Hsu" width=33% height=10%> <figcaption> An actual ocean sunfish. Photo by Fred Hsu </figcaption> </figure> Here's an image of my CAD model. I ended up modeling the sunfish first then using the join function to cut the model out of an extruded rectangle to make the negative. <figure> <img src="./Ocean Sunfish Model.png" alt="Image of my ocean sunfish which looks very derpy in comparison" width=33% height=10%> <img src="./Ocean Sunfish Model Negative.png" alt="Image of my ocean sunfish as a negative in a block" width=33% height=9%> <figcaption> My ocean sunfish, both the positive model and negative. Design by me </figcaption> </figure> I had a lot of help troubleshooting the CNC machine with Victor and Bobby. Shoutout Victor, Bobby, and Alice for helping me get through the CNC process while I was suppose to be proctoring the Chem 27 exam (don't worry they had 7 other proctors). The CNC itself only took 12 minutes and turned out really nicely! (It did take 30 minutes to troubleshoot). We only used the 1/8th ball bit and the resolution was pretty good. <figure> <img src="./Victor and Bobby with CNC.JPG" alt="Victor and Bobby with CNC" width=33% height=10%> <img src="./Finished CNC Mold Ocean Sunfish.JPG" alt="The mold cut out of wax" width=33% height=10%> <figcaption> Victor and Bobby with the CNC (L) and the ocean sunfish mold in wax (R) </figcaption> </figure> Alice and Bobby tried to find mold release to no avail, so I just sent it. Alice and I mixed the plaster with water until it was pancake batter consistency and added a bit of black acrylic paint to make the plaster more grey. I first poured some plaster to 80% capacity then added in the pushpin which fit nicely. <figure> <img src="./Pushpin in mold.JPG" alt="The pushpin and mold line up perfectly in height" width=30% height=10%> <img src="./Just poured mold.JPG" alt="Gloopy plaster in mold" width=30% height=10%> <img src="./Partially set mold.JPG" alt="The somewhat set mold with the pushpin outline visible" width=30% height=10%> <figcaption> The pushpin in the mold (L), the just poured plaster (C), and the somewhat set plaster (R) </figcaption> </figure> Now came the fun part--seeing if I could remove the mold at an unspecified time well into the morning (spoiler alert: the plaster was not fully set). I started by carving around the sunfish with a toothpick to not disturb the plaster and tried prying up after digging 1 mm in. Unsurprisingly the only thing that happened was taking some of the fin off. After carving away like I was the CNC machine, I was finally able to wiggle the sunfish loose. The parts agains the wax turned out pretty well even if there are a few air bubbles and only the sides got damaged. However, this mold is not super usable again due to the damage from getting the sunfish out. <figure> <img src="./Carving sunfish out.JPG" alt="The pushpin and mold line up perfectly in height" width=30% height=10%> <img src="./Sunfish mold post carving.JPG" alt="Gloopy plaster in mold" width=30% height=10%> <figcaption> Carving the sunfish out (L) and sunfish mold post carving (R) </figcaption> </figure> This process took the better part of 40 minutes and damage to the sunfish, but finally it was freed! Yes there was some damage, but I am overall quite happy with how the sunfish turned out. I can grab it comfortably and think it will be functional as a pushpin. <figure> <img src="./Freedom to swim.JPG" alt="Sunfish product in mold" width=30% height=10%> <img src="./Sunfish in the white box.JPG" alt="Sunfish against white background" width=30% height=10%> <img src="./Sunfish damage.JPG" alt="The rough edges of the sunfish due to damage when removing from mold" width=30% height=10%> <figcaption> Freedom! (L), the sunfish against a white background (C), and damage to the sunfish (R) </figcaption> </figure> <br><br> Here's the .stl file if you want to make it yourself. I'd recommend trying mold release though because the ocean sunfish took a long time to remove. <p class="margin"> </p> <div class="flexrow"> <a id="btn" href="../06_inputs/Ocean Sunfish.zip" download>Download the Ocean Sunfish .stl </a> </div> <p class="margin"> </p> </div>
