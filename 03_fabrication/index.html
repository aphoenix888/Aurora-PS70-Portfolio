<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3>Week 3: Hand Tools and Fabrication</h3> This week, we learned how to solder and soldered our Xiao microcontrollers together. I used this to power my kinetic sculpture. <br> <br> <h4>Kinetic Sculpture</h4> For my kinetic sculpture, I initially wanted to make a jellyfish lava lamp. My first thought was to use a peristaltic pump to blast pulses of water, but a lack of ability to turn the pump on and off made me pivot to a mechanical up and down inspired by the snail cam in the gear workshop. <br> After settling on this design, I started sketching ideas and figuring out what I needed to cad. <figure> <img src="./Jellyfish Sketch.jpg" alt="Fill in later." width=37% height=13%> <figcaption> Here are some sketches of the kinetic sculpture in varying stages of brainstorming. For simplicity (and because I like sleeping) I decided to just attach the jellyfish to the pole. </figcaption> </figure> <br> Here is the prototype of gears out of cardboard. <figure> <img src="./Gear prototyping jellyfish.JPG" alt="Two cardboard gears, one big and one small, and snail cames with yellow motor and wooden dowel" width=37% height=13%> <figcaption> The large gear is to decrease the speed the jellyfish goes up and down. I used a flat stick instead of a dowel for connecting the gear to the snail cams in order to prevent slippage. </figcaption> </figure> After some testing, I realized I forgot to to align the gears vertically and the small gear on the motor was catching on the box. This led to a new base design. The gear attachment to the motor was also failing and spinning, so I decided to print them out of wood. At this point, there was a cardboard shortage so I made the gearbox out of wood too. I actually really liked using wood and hope to make a nicer looking base out of wood. At this iteration, the main issues were kerf related because the tabs did not stay firmly in the base holes, knocking the gears out of alignment. Because I am hesitant to glue things down, particularly the motor casing, I used masking tape to secure the components to the base. The circuit only has the motor powered by the 5V Xiao pin. <figure> <img src="./Gearside Jellyfish Whitebox.JPG" alt="The assembled jellyfish kinetic sculpture." width=37% height=13%> <figcaption> The overall kinetic sculpture, masking tape included. </figcaption> </figure> <iframe src="https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Iyy7ae8kx535rpcXLVf04RizCBibY460/preview" width="640" height="480"></iframe> <br> Here is the jellyfish moving! <br> <br> Shoutout Bobby for a lesson in how gears work and Victor for late Science Center office hours.
